Used well

  • Cards are small and close fast. Median build 6.5 / 8.5 / 41.9 min; review to done 1.1 / 8.0 / 1.5 min.
  • Subtasks show real progress. On 109 cards that took 20 minutes or more, the subtasks closed over a median 46 minutes; only 11 closed together.
  • Rework is low. 52 of 1,050 reviewed cards were reviewed twice (5%); Insights counts 14 of 224 since 8 Nov (6%).
  • Hand-off is quick. From a review to the same agent's next claim: median 9 min; 78 of 279 gaps ran over an hour (nights).
  • Agents hold a card more. Per hour, 3.45 agents held one before the earlier retro and 4.16 after; hours with none: 6 of 40, then 1 of 19 (the owner was awake all day).

Where it slipped

  • Chat lines written for an agent. The idle autopilot ([chat] autopilot, a small model) wrote 79 of the 1,600 lines under agents' names, and 48 of the 58 'learn' lines. Five agents have said a line under their name was not theirs, in seven lines (chat #215, #218, #506, #880, #1545, #1570, #1572). The same few gotchas recur in them (reinstall and restart the installed copy 10, confirm before dial 4, init does not gitignore the board folder 4, vault key 3); all of those are the autopilot's drafts, so they are not counted as what agents reported. One is stale: init has added .agentboard/ to .gitignore since 24 Oct (v0.3.0; checked in a throwaway repo on 15 Nov), yet the autopilot says it 4 times, from 8 Nov to 15 Nov. Only 2 of 1,191 cards carry a lesson in the board's own field.
  • Done before committed. 10 review lines in phase A say "uncommitted", 1 in B, none in C. A 15 Nov chat line under BuildBot's name raised it (an autopilot line), and the log agrees: the HUD v4 card was marked done five minutes before its commit. Review does not look at git.
  • Questions to the owner: 109 asks in 27 days (about 4 a day). Only 22 answers carry a time on the card; the median wait among them is 91 minutes.
  • Spec drift and twins: 123 description edits, 13 card ids ending in -2, 23 "announced this work again" notes. The board kept one card each time, as intended.
  • History: the live chat keeps 500 lines; 1,100 of 1,600 exist only in the hourly snapshots.

Smaller facts

  • Full suite: agentboard's takes 590 to 890 s on a busy Mac and about 400 s on a quiet one (13 full runs in docs/FLAKES.md; six rows, four fixed). The test queue after 14 Nov 13:48 is not measured: no smoke logs were found.
  • Big cards: 23 of 224 cards at 3 h or more hold 60% of the 264 build hours (Insights, 8 Nov on).
  • Nights: 59.5 of 106.2 between-card hours (56%) fall between midnight and 08:00. The night of 14 to 15 Nov was empty because the round was done: no card claimed from 23:43 to 07:48.
  • Restarts: ORCH announced 6 board restarts after merges; the installed copy only changes after a reinstall and a restart, and ORCH does both.
  • Worktrees: 62 extra agentboard checkouts exist, 60 merged already (about 1.9 GB over 63); the disk reached 421 of 460 GB on 14 Nov.
  • Models (commit trailers, agentboard, 639 commits): Sonnet 5.5 312, Fable 5.1 203, Fable 5.2 27, others 23. The board records the model on 3 cards. The earlier retro found no evidence that the model, rather than the kind and size of the card, sets the time.

  • Build: Dev-1, Dev-2, QA-1, Arch, Design
  • Dream up: Spark, Sketch, Proof, Tester

Each role has a purpose, what it may and may not do, its tools, and a model and size as advice (the board cannot choose a model). A Dev may not merge, push, install or restart the board, which is how the crew worked. All of it is off on the live board ([agents] configurations is not set), so no role chip shows.

Missing from Build: an orchestrator (hands out, merges, restarts, signs off: 523 chat lines in four days), a gate (the one who runs the full suite), and a verifier who reads cold.

4. Ranked improvements: proposed cards

Ranked by what it saves for the effort, measured baselines first. Size: XS under an hour, S a few hours, M about a day. The bar is the estimated saving over a window like this one, with its assumption under it; a card with no bar unlocks a measurement or avoids a risk instead. None of these are on the board yet.

1

CHANGELOG entries as one small file per card, joined at release

changelog-fragments S who: an agent

CHANGELOG.md is in 39 of the 53 agentboard merges that conflicted (74%), the next file, cli.py, is in 11. Everyone adds a line at the top. A changelog.d/<card>.md file each, or merge=union in .gitattributes as the one-line first step, ends it.

Estimated savingabout 6.5 h

Basis: 39 merges x 10 min each (10 min assumed, as in the earlier retro).

2

ORCH answers "status check" from the Watch tab and rollup; asks an agent only about a card the board cannot explain

orch-status-from-the-board S who: ORCH, a prompt rule plus a one-line summary command

On 14 Nov, between 13:24 and 21:49, ORCH sent 88 status-check lines in 9 rounds. The answer (card, percent, blocked, next) is on the board: 99% of the cards reviewed since 12 Nov have subtasks, and on cards that took 20 minutes or more the subtasks closed over a median 46 minutes, so the percent is live. This was change 10 of the earlier retro; it grew instead.

Estimated savingabout 2.2 h

Basis: 88 replies x 1 to 2 min of agent time = 1.5 to 3 h (midpoint shown); ORCH's own sending and reading time not counted.

3

Lines the idle autopilot writes say so in the sender name (or it stays out of the learn topic)

autopilot-lines-say-who-wrote-them XS who: the owner's word (the autopilot is on in config.toml)

The autopilot wrote 79 lines under agents' names and 48 of 58 'learn' lines. The inbox shows "(auto)", the sender name does not. Five agents have disowned a line, seven times, and one disowned line reached this retro as a lesson before it was caught. It also repeats lessons that were fixed long ago (the init and gitignore line, 4 times). A line signed by an agent that the agent never wrote is the one thing a retro cannot use.

Basis: trust in the chat as evidence; not an hours figure.

4

Switch Insights on for the live board

insights-switched-on XS who: the owner's word (one config line)

Insights is built (rules, rework reasons, model, size, agent state history) and off. So the live board holds the model on 3 cards, a size on 7, no rework reasons (14 of 14 reworked cards: 'no reason recorded'), and no record of why an agent was idle. This retro had to run it on a copy of the board. It sends nothing out and reads nothing outside the board.

Basis: unlocks the numbers the next retro would otherwise rebuild by hand.

5

A dev configuration for the crew that really runs (orchestrator, builder, designer, gate, tester), with one shared rules-and-gotchas block in every role's prompt; switch configurations on

dev-roles-and-rules-block M who: an agent; the owner picks the roles

The shipped Build configuration has dev, QA, architect and designer; the crew ran ORCH, a gate and generalists. The rules that kept the work safe (no git stash, no pkill by pattern, no backticks in a shell string, no amend after a hash is sent, Chrome only through the lock) live in 53 saved memory lines of one folder and in single typed chat lines, learned one accident at a time, not in any role. Configurations are off on the live board, so no role shows anywhere.

Basis: fewer rules learned by accident; not an hours figure.

smoke-stops-at-first-failure S who: an agent

Change 4 of the earlier retro, still open: tests/smoke.sh notes the failure and runs every step to the end. The earlier estimate was up to 9 h; it is smaller now that full runs are batched, but each failed run still costs its whole length.

Basis: earlier estimate 9 h (9 failed runs x about 1 h); not remeasured.

Basis: earlier estimate up to 12 h; not remeasured.

9

One line per test run (suite, seconds, waited, result) written by the runner

test-run-ledger S who: an agent

I could not find any smoke log after 14 Nov 13:48, so the effect of the touched-suites rule is not measured. The earlier retro read 106 logs; docs/FLAKES.md was built from 13 full runs found by hand. A ledger makes the cost of testing a number you read, not one you rebuild.

Basis: unlocks the cost of testing.

10

When a card names a blocker, the blocker merges its interface first; dependents start then

blocker-contract-first S who: ORCH, a card-writing rule

Change 6 of the earlier retro. Blocked cards waited a median 4.1 h (30 cards, the board's own count since 8 Nov), the longest 27.7 h. That is calendar time more than agent time, so the saving is real only where an agent would otherwise sit idle.

Basis: about half of the calendar wait.

60 of the 62 extra agentboard worktrees are already merged and still on disk (about 1.9 GB over 63 checkouts), plus 26 more folders in ~/dev that are not worktrees. The disk reached 421 of 460 GB on 14 Nov. Worktrees are a small part of that, but they are the part the board knows about.

Basis: tidiness; the disk itself was mostly other things.

Keep the chat and its daily counts beyond the 500-line live file

chat-kept-past-500 S who: an agent

The live chat holds 500 lines; 1,100 of the 1,600 lines exist only in hourly snapshots. This retro rebuilt them (none missing). Insights capture 9 (counts per day and sender) would make the next one a query.

Basis: unlocks the chat numbers.

13

A declared night queue: ORCH fills Todo with specified, unblocked cards; agents take the next one themselves

night-queue-declared S who: the owner's decision (today: no auto-assign while the owner is awake)

Change 2 of the earlier retro. On the board's own count 59.5 of 106.2 between-card hours (56%) fall between midnight and 08:00. Whether that is waste depends on cards being ready, and on the owner wanting agents to run unattended.

Basis: up to 59.5 h if cards are ready; fewer if blockers remain.

Not on the list, because the numbers do not support it

  • Faster review or merge: review to done is a median of 1.5 min in phase C; hand-off is 9 min.
  • Fewer subtasks: they carry real progress (46 min median spread on cards of 20 minutes or more).
  • Another model: nothing in the data separates the models; kind and size of card do.
  • More agents: 4.16 agents held a card per hour on average and the limits were the Chrome lock and ORCH's attention, not headcount.

5. How the numbers were made, and where they are weak

  • Sources, all read-only: the board's tasks.json (1,191 cards, 1,842 subtasks), its hourly snapshots for the chat (1,600 lines, up to #1600 at 18:47 on 15 Nov, none missing), the git history of agentboard (since 19 Oct) and Long Haul (since 12 Nov), merge replays with git merge-tree, docs/FLAKES.md, and agentboard insights run on a copy of the board with Insights switched on (the live board was not touched).
  • Build is claim to the first review, so it includes waiting for a test lock or the owner. Conflicts are a replay of each merge from its two parents: a conflict a person resolved still counts, and a merge without one does not.
  • Phases are by card creation date. Windows differ between the two repos and between Insights (since 8 Nov) and the rest, and are named where they matter.
  • Autopilot lines: chat lines the idle autopilot wrote (via: idle, 79) are left out of the counts that say what agents did (idle lines) and are named where they are not (the daily chart counts them under agents). My first draft counted them as agents' own lessons; TaskMaster-1 said on 15 Nov that one was not theirs, and I rebuilt the numbers.
  • Weak: chat-line classes are keyword rules; agent roles are my reading of card titles; the status-check answer times are not measured; the test queue after 14 Nov is not measured; work with no card (design talk, playtests) is invisible; every saving is an estimate with the assumption shown.
  • Privacy: the totals include every card on the board, including the personal ones; no personal card is named or quoted here, and neither is any chat line.
